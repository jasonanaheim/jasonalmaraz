Messenger Kids

Keeping the purpose of a feature while finding a practical path to launch.

Role / Product ManagerCodazen / Staffed at MetaFocus / Scope & delivery

01 / The challenge

The Messenger Kids website helped explain the product to parents. As an external agency continued refining the designs, a testimonial carousel was added to the scope, introducing engineering work that put the initial delivery date at risk.

02 / My contribution

I reviewed the earlier designs to understand the scope change, worked with engineering to estimate the additional effort, and explained the delivery implications to the client.

03 / A key decision

I proposed launching with a single testimonial card and adding the carousel in a second phase. This preserved the testimonial content while giving engineering a smaller initial scope. I coordinated the two phases with the client and engineering team.

04 / The outcome

We met the initial delivery date with the testimonial card, then built the carousel in the second phase. The tradeoff was that visitors initially saw one testimonial rather than being able to browse several.

05 / What I learned

Separating a feature’s purpose from its proposed implementation can reveal a simpler first release. Next time, I would establish a clearer design approval checkpoint to identify scope changes earlier.
