webDiplomacy

Redesigning the map and gameplay interface, starting with what players needed to complete a turn.

Role / Product ManagerCodazen / Staffed at MetaFocus / Interface & prioritization

01 / The project

At Codazen, I worked on the redesign of webDiplomacy’s map and gameplay interface. Diplomacy is a strategy game where players negotiate, form alliances, and submit moves that play out at the same time. The platform was used in Meta AI’s CICERO research. My role focused on the interface: clarifying requirements, prioritizing what we built first, and working with design and engineering to deliver it.

02 / What made it challenging

I joined after the project had already been estimated. As we looked closer, it became clear that updating the interface meant understanding how it connected to the existing game. Players still needed to sign in, understand the game state, and enter their orders correctly. Those dependencies affected both the scope and the delivery plan.

03 / Where I started

I needed to understand how people actually played before I could help prioritize the experience. I learned the rules and worked with the game’s owner to walk through movement, support orders, and the different phases of a game. Alongside engineering, I documented the open questions: what could we reuse, what needed additional work, and what had to function for someone to complete a turn?

04 / The decision

I prioritized a low-fidelity, playable desktop MVP. We needed to validate the core interactions with the existing game engine before expanding into mobile and further visual polish. The tradeoff was accepting a less polished first version and delaying parts of the broader experience. That gave us a clear initial goal: could someone use the new interface to play?

05 / How I moved it forward

I translated that scope into user stories and milestones, then worked with the owner, designers, and engineers to align on the sequence. I also tested the MVP myself. Going through the gameplay helped me check whether the essential interactions worked, beyond whether the interface matched the designs.

06 / What shipped

The team shipped the redesigned webDiplomacy interface. My contribution was helping define the initial scope, clarify the integration requirements, and organize delivery around a playable experience. CICERO’s research results belong to the broader Meta AI initiative; my work was on the interface and its delivery.

07 / What I’d do differently

I would investigate the functional dependencies earlier, before committing to the full delivery estimate. For a redesign like this, I’d establish an initial discovery milestone around authentication, game state, and order entry. The biggest lesson for me was to validate the essential experience early. A redesigned map only works if people can still use it to play.
